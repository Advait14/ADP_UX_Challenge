# Problem and Research

## Problem framing

Recruiters handling high-volume Customer Support Associate applications spend time repeatedly reviewing applications, checking minimum qualifications, conducting initial outreach, evaluating answers, and deciding who progresses. This can delay candidates, create inconsistent first-level review, and make strong but non-traditional candidates easier to miss.

## Evidence types

### Known from the assessment brief

- A recruiter screens more than 25 candidates daily for customer support and call-center roles.
- The manual workflow includes application review, minimum qualification checks, outreach, pre-screening, evaluation, and progression decisions.
- The expected solution must retain recruiter oversight and address transparency, fairness, scalability, candidate experience, and privacy.

### Practitioner input shared for this assessment

- High application volumes make manual screening difficult.
- Recruiters manage multiple roles, contributing to delayed status communication.
- A role scorecard and agreed criteria reduce mismatches between recruiter and hiring-manager expectations.
- Matching only direct skills can overlook transferable evidence; uncertain cases need human review.

### Secondary research direction

NIST’s AI Risk Management Framework describes trustworthy AI characteristics including transparency, explainability, privacy, and harmful-bias management. It also stresses defined human-AI roles and documentation. The prototype applies this by making AI recommendations advisory, exposing evidence and uncertainty, recording recruiter decisions, and excluding irrelevant attributes from the stated screening inputs.

The U.S. EEOC has warned that automated employment systems can create disability-discrimination risks and recommends accommodation processes. The prototype therefore includes an alternative-format / escalation route as a design consideration, not as a legal compliance claim.

Greenhouse’s 2023 candidate survey reports that communication is an important part of the candidate experience. The prototype uses recruiter-approved status-update drafts to address avoidable silence without allowing automatic consequential messaging.

## Assumptions to validate

- Recruiters will understand a concise evidence summary faster than a raw score.
- Recruiters will use a structured assessment for low-confidence cases rather than reject them.
- Hiring managers need a compact shortlist rationale, not recruiter-queue access.
